# COMFAR-compatible financial model: engineering spec

- Status: draft for EPIC-33/34/37 (2026-10-02)
- Owner decision: the feasibility methodology is UNIDO with the COMFAR III model (OQ-35); every economic assumption is entered per project by the user (no platform defaults).
- Reference: _COMFAR III Expert Reference Manual_ (UNIDO, 2003). The owner's copy is kept locally in `references/comfar-iii/` (git-ignored; licensed material, never committed). Chapter references below (e.g. "XI.K") point into that manual.
- This spec restates the model in our own terms for implementation in `@roshd/financial-engine` (ADR-0009). Where we deliberately differ from COMFAR, it says so.

## 1. Time and periods

- **Planning horizon** = construction phase + production phase (+ one "scrap" year after production for residual values). (VII.G)
- **Implementation (ST-34.02, `packages/financial-engine/src/model/horizon.ts`, `planHorizon`):** inputs are the month construction starts in (Solar Hijri or Gregorian; only the month count matters), the **month of balance** (last month of the financial year, e.g. Esfand), the construction phase (number of equal periods of 1, 3, 6 or 12 months; may be 0), an optional **start-up phase** of periodic planning at the start of production (equal periods, at most 24 months) and the **number of production years**. Production starts the month after construction and ends on the n-th balance date, so the first production year is shortened when production does not start right after a balance date (the rows of the manual's table 5 are tests). After the start-up phase every period runs to the next balance date. The horizon is at most 600 months; every period has a 30/360 end-day index for loans. `balanceYears` lists the financial years of production with their months (the first may be partial) and the period that contains each balance date, where yearly amounts such as depreciation are booked. Start-up periods are not cut at balance dates: e.g. half-year start-up periods from April with a December balance month straddle the first balance dates, and a 24-month start-up after a partial first year reaches into a third financial year; the engine accepts this and books a year's amounts in the period that contains its balance date (OQ-39).
- **Flows occur on the last day of the period** in which they are defined, unless a loan rule says otherwise. (XI intro)
- **Day-count:** 360-day year, 30-day months. Interest for a sub-interval uses `m/12 + d/360` (whole months plus excess days). (XI.D, XI.M.2)
- **Partial first year:** inflation/escalation entered for the partial year only; fixed costs of a production period shorter than a year scale by `m/12`. (XI.C, XI.J)

## 2. Currencies, inflation, escalation (XI.A–C)

Three currencies: **input** (I, any item may be entered in it), **local** (L, calculation currency), **accounting** (A, reporting currency, with a display unit such as "million rials").

- Without inflation: `P_A = P_I × ER_LI × ER_AL` (constant rates).
- With inflation (user enables per project):
  - relative inflation factor for year j: `PR_j = Π_{i=1..j-1} (1+R_L,i)/(1+R_I,i)`; first year not inflated;
  - current exchange rate `ER_LI,j = PR_j × ER_LI,0` (devaluation follows relative inflation).
  - **Our extension:** the user may instead enter the exchange-rate path directly; the derived rate is one option, not the rule.
- Item price escalation `E` is inflation of the item **relative to** its currency's inflation; current price compounds `(1 + R_j + E_j)` year by year (with a first-year escalator option). Escalation must not double-count inflation; the UI explains this.
- Calculation in L; conversion to A at the **initial** A/L rate, then divided by the display unit.
- **Foreign-currency loans** are computed in their own currency and converted at the current rate; the restatement creates an exchange gain/loss `ADJ_j = B_{j+1} − (B_j + D_j − R_j + CI_j)` (in L), shown in the balance sheet and debt service. (XI.B, XI.M.2)
- **Asset revaluation** (optional, with inflation): `V_j = V_i × FI_j / FI_i`, `FI_j = Π(1+R)`; depreciation on revalued amounts; the uplift is a separate "revaluation adjustment" in net worth. (XI.B)

### 2.1 Implementation rules (ST-33.06, `packages/financial-engine/src/indexation.ts`)

All paths are per year ending on a balance date; for a partial first year the user enters the rate of that partial year (nothing is scaled). Year-level factors are mapped to project periods by the model layer (ST-34).

- **Current prices:** `F_1 = 1 + R_1 + ((1 + E_1)^e − 1)`, `F_j = F_{j−1} × (1 + R_j + E_j)`, current price = entered price × `F_j`. Inflation and escalation are added inside a year, as printed. Escalation is required (one rate or one per year; "0" is an explicit choice) and so is the first-year escalator `e` (whole number ≥ 0).
- **Timing of year 1 (the manual is inconsistent, OQ-39):** the price formula inflates year 1 (`R_1` is in `F_1`), while the exchange-rate factor `PR_j` and the revaluation index `FI_j` start in year 2 ("first year not inflated"). The engine follows each formula as printed: entered prices are prices at the start of the horizon with flows at the end of the year, and the exchange rate and asset index of year 1 equal their initial values. The acceptance study (ST-36.01) checks this against COMFAR itself.
- **Exchange rates:** either entered directly per year (our extension) or derived: `PR_1 = 1`, `PR_j = Π_{i<j} (1 + R_L,i)/(1 + R_I,i)`, `ER_j = PR_j × ER_0` (local units per foreign unit). The initial rate is required.
- **Own index paths (our extension):** a category may follow a user-entered index (e.g. a price index); factors are `level_j / level_base`.
- **Foreign-currency loans:** the schedule is computed in the loan currency (ST-33.05) and restated per period (when the periods carry the schedule's closing balance, as `loanPeriods` does, that balance is used instead of the running sum of the rounded period flows, so a repaid loan ends at exactly 0): flows and the closing balance at the period's rate, the opening balance equal to the previous local closing balance (the first one at the first period's rate), and `ADJ_j = closing − (opening + D − R + CI)` in local currency (positive = exchange loss), computed in the equivalent form `foreign opening balance × ER_j − local opening balance`, so a constant rate books exactly 0. Interest and fees are converted at the period's rate. Converting the opening balance of the first period at that period's rate is our reading (OQ-39): with derived rates `ER_1 = ER_0`, so nothing is lost; with a directly entered path and a starting balance (expansion projects), the move from `ER_0` to `ER_1` on that balance is not booked as an adjustment.

### 3.2 Project assumptions and templates (ST-34.01)

- **Schema:** `projectAssumptionsSchema` in `@roshd/validation` (`src/financial-model.ts`), shared by the API and the web editor, with Persian messages. It holds the horizon (calendar for display, construction start month, month of balance, construction phase, start-up phase and production years as in §1; the engine's own checks run in the schema and show their Persian message at the field), the currencies (local, up to ten foreign, reporting currency and display unit such as million rials), the inflation choice and the assumptions. Nothing has a default.
- **Assumption:** key (e.g. `discountRate`, `inflation.IRR`, `exchangeRate.USD`, `custom.landPrice`), label, unit, **either** one value **or** a per-period path (one value per period of `planHorizon`: construction, start-up and production years), and free-text source and as-of date. Numbers are decimal strings; Persian digits and «٫» are accepted, and a comma or «٬» only as a thousands separator between groups of three digits (a first group starting with 0, as in «0,125», is refused rather than read as 125; spaces are not separators).
- **Drafts save incomplete.** `checkAssumptionsForCalculation` lists what blocks a calculation, one field-level Persian message per missing assumption: the discount rate; an exchange rate for every foreign currency (a value alone is the initial rate — constant, or derived from relative inflation when inflation is on, COMFAR rule — a path is the user's own exchange-rate path); and, with inflation on, an inflation rate for every currency. Later sections (loans, tax, production) add their own required inputs.
- **Personal templates:** a user may save a set of assumptions as a template (`/api/v1/assumption-templates`, owner only, 404 for everyone else including staff, at most 50 per user, audit-logged) and copy it into another project with `applyAssumptionTemplate` (same keys replaced, others added, the rest untouched). Templates are applied only on the user's request; the platform ships none. The copy action in the editor arrives with the financial-model module (ST-34.06/34.07).

### 3.3 Stored model and calculation runs (ST-34.06, `apps/api/src/modules/financial-model`)

- **Model:** title, owner, optional assigned expert, and the inputs as JSON in the shape of the engine's `ProjectInput` (`projectInputSchema` in `@roshd/validation`, schema version 1). A model is a draft: it may be saved incomplete. Every save bumps `version`; the editor sends the version it loaded and a save on top of a newer one is refused (409), so two editors cannot overwrite each other silently.
- **Calculation:** `POST /financial-models/:id/runs` checks the saved inputs with `projectInputSchema` (shape, sizes, number format; Persian digits are normalised) and runs `projectModel`. Missing or invalid inputs are returned field by field (`inputs.…`), the engine's own errors with their Persian message; nothing is stored then.
- **Run (ADR-0009 §6):** number within the model, version of the model it was taken from, the validated input, the SHA-256 of its canonical JSON (keys sorted), `MODEL_VERSION`, results (every schedule and statement), warnings, the COMFAR defaults used, user and time. A run is never recalculated or changed — the API has no route for it and a database trigger refuses the update; a new calculation is a new run. The engine runs in a worker thread, one calculation at a time per API process, so a large model never blocks other requests; a calculation that takes longer than 5 seconds (or outgrows the worker's memory) is stopped and refused, at most 8 calculations are in the queue, the running one included (then 503), and a user has one calculation in it at a time, so the longest wait is about 40 seconds. Further limits: a rough size of the model (project periods × lines, products and cost centres, plus the allocation of indirect costs) at most 20 000, results at most 4 million characters of JSON (stored whole), 10 calculation requests per user and minute (valid or not; at most 50 validation messages are returned), 50 runs per model and 50 models per owner.
- **Approval:** the assigned expert or staff approve a run once; the approval (who, when) cannot be changed or withdrawn. An approved run cannot be deleted, so neither can its model (a database trigger refuses it, also for the cascade from a deleted user account: such an account has to be anonymised instead). Nobody approves a run of a model they own, and the owner cannot be assigned as the expert of their own model. Four eyes (owner decision of 2026-10-03, OQ-40): the user who calculated a run cannot approve it, whatever their role; the database refuses it as well.
- **Access:** the owner, the expert assigned to the model (must hold `financial-models:work`; the `expert` role has it) and staff with `financial-models:manage` (full access to every model: read, edit, calculate, assign the expert, approve). For anyone else the model and its runs do not exist (404). Owner decision of 2026-10-03: the expert is assigned on the model itself until the feasibility project exists (ST-35.01), then the assignment moves there. Every create, save, delete, assignment, calculation and approval is in the audit log.
- **Open:** the per-project assumptions of §3.2 (with source and as-of date) are not yet linked to the stored inputs; the editor (ST-34.07) does that. The per-user limit and the calculation queue are kept in the memory of each API process. One user can still store up to 50 × 50 runs; a per-owner storage quota is not implemented.

### 3.4 Editor of the inputs (ST-34.07, `apps/web/src/components/models`, `apps/web/src/lib/model-editor`)

- The draft the editor saves **is** the engine's input (`projectInputSchema`), possibly incomplete; there is no second format. The same schema decides in the browser and on the server whether a calculation can run.
- Sections: assumptions (horizon, currencies and exchange rates, inflation, discounting, tax, conventions of the statements), investment (items, sale of assets, allowances), financing (equity with dividend conditions, profit distribution, loans), production and sales, costs (items and cost centres), working capital (every coverage in one place), starting balance sheet (ST-34.11: the project is marked as the expansion or rehabilitation of an existing enterprise and its balances of §4.14 are entered; leaving the expansion asks first and removes them).
- Numbers are shown with Persian digits and «٬» / «٫» and stored as canonical decimal strings; rates and shares are shown in percent and stored as fractions, converted by moving the decimal point in the text (no floating point). What is not a number is kept as typed and reported at the field.
- Tables have one column per project period, project year (inflation, escalation) or production year (tax rates, dividends). The planning horizon is edited as a whole and takes effect only when the user applies it; before that the editor says how many periods lose their values. On applying, a period keeps the values of the old period that covers **the same time**: the same months counted from the start of construction (construction periods) or from the start of production (start-up and production periods). A construction phase longer by whole years therefore moves the production programme with the start of production; a change that moves the start of production inside the financial year (half a year more of construction, another start month) makes the first production year partial, so no production period covers the same time and none keeps its values; a start-up phase added in the first year replaces that year and leaves the later years; a change of the month of balance or of a period length keeps nothing of the periods it changes. Inputs that name a period (start of depreciation, production interval, sale of an asset) follow its first or last day, or are asked again when no period begins or ends there. Series per project year and per production year keep their order, and the dates of loans (months from the start of the project) are not moved — the editor tells the user to review them. New periods get 0 for amounts and quantities and an empty cell for rates and prices. No economic value is ever filled in.
- Loan dates are entered as month numbers from the start of the project and stored as 30/360 day indices (month end = month × 30; a rate applies from the first day of its month).
- A name other inputs refer to is written when its field is left, and a name another item already has is refused, so references stay unambiguous. Renaming renames every reference (a product in its costs, allocation shares and cost centres; a contribution in its dividend conditions; an investment item in its sale; an investment item, cost item, product, loan or contribution in its starting balance; the local currency in every input entered in it). Removing an item asks first and removes what refers to it (a product with its direct costs, any item with its starting balance).
- `notes` (optional, in the input): source and as-of date of an assumption, keyed by the path of the input (`exchangeRates.USD`, `inflation.IRR`, `statements.discounting`, `statements.tax`). They are stored with the run and ignored by the engine.
- Saving: a moment after every change, on top of the loaded version. A 409 is first checked against the server: when it holds exactly what this editor sent — in this save or in any earlier one that got no answer since the last accepted save — nobody else changed the model and the editor goes on from the server's version; otherwise saving stops, and the user may download a copy of the page before loading the newer version. While the typed title is not valid, the inputs are saved with the last valid title. "Store a calculation run" saves first and then asks the server to calculate the saved inputs.
- The live calculation runs `projectModel` in a web worker and shows the indicators only; the statements are the subject of ST-34.08. It is never stored: a run exists only when the server calculated it. While the result on screen is older than the inputs, it is dimmed and a run cannot be stored.
- Escalation of prices is required with inflation and optional at constant prices (a real price change); the days of coverage keep their unit, and changing the unit asks for the value again.
- The menu entry is shown to holders of `financial-models:work` or `financial-models:manage` (OQ-38 is still open for members).

### 3.5 Result views (ST-34.08, `apps/web/src/components/models/results`)

- A stored run is shown from its own data: `results` (the engine's `ProjectModel`), `warnings`, `defaultsUsed`, and the horizon and local currency of its `input` snapshot. Nothing is recalculated to show a statement, so the page shows exactly what was stored — also after the model or the engine changed. Runs are never changed and may be older than the page: a part whose stored result has another shape shows a plain message instead of partial figures (the other parts stay readable), and a snapshot the current input schema no longer accepts still shows its tables but offers no scenario or sensitivity analysis.
- Tables (`@roshd/financial-report/tables`, shared with the exports of §5.2): income statement, cash flow for financial planning (with the labelled automatic coverage lines), balance sheet, discounted cash flows of total capital and equity (with the residual value in its own row, in the year after production when it returns there), and ratios with break-even and debt-service coverage per period. Lines keep COMFAR's order; totals are the engine's lines, never sums made by the page.
- A run with shares of the net worth (§4.15) shows the indicators of every shareholder on the summary, each with its own warnings, and a discounted cash flow table per shareholder; the exports have the same blocks and tables.
- A run of an expansion project (§4.14) shows the starting balance sheet as a first column «پیش از طرح» of the balance sheet, and the charge of the starting balance as a first column of the discounted cash flows; the exports show the same columns and list the entered balances with the inputs. Incremental analysis has its own part of the page (§7.2).
- Amounts are shown in a display unit chosen on the page (units, thousands, millions, billions of the local currency) by moving the decimal point of the stored value and rounding for display only.
- A warning that is about one indicator of one basis (IRR, MIRR, payback, dynamic payback, NPV ratio) is shown next to that indicator; the others are listed together. An indicator that cannot be computed reads «ندارد», never 0.
- Charts are plain SVG and read right to left like the tables. Each has a title, a description and the same figures as a table directly under it (the cumulative cash flow in a collapsible table, the tornado in the sensitivity table). On a narrow screen a chart keeps a readable size and scrolls sideways.
- Scenarios and sensitivity (§7.1) are calculated in a web worker on the run's input snapshot. The variables are COMFAR's global changes — sales price and quantity, price and quantity of production costs, fixed investment, exchange rate, inflation, discount rate — offered only when the model has such inputs. Changes are relative (10 % more of a 20 % rate is 22 %), which the page says. The percentages are always typed by the user; none is suggested. A sensitivity step lies above −100 % and at most +1000 %, and one analysis asks for at most 48 model runs (variables × steps); a running analysis can be stopped, its inputs are locked while it runs, and a result is removed as soon as its inputs change. What was typed and calculated stays while another part of the page is open, but nothing is stored: it is an analysis of the run on screen.
- Approval: the API returns `canApprove` with every run (assigned expert or staff, not the model's owner, not the user who calculated the run, run not approved yet) and does not return the run's creator. The page asks before approving, because an approval cannot be undone.

## 4. Calculation rules

### 4.1 Discounting, NPV, IRR, MIRR (XI.D–E)

- Factor for `m` months from the reference date: `f = (1 + d)^(m/12)`; discounted amount `A / f`.
- `NPV = Σ A_j / f_j + SV / f_n`. When the reference date is the end of the first year, first-year amounts are not discounted (exponents shift by one).
- IRR: rate(s) where NPV = 0. The number of roots can equal the number of sign changes; the engine reports **all roots found or none**, never picks one silently (warning in Persian).
- MIRR rates default to the IRR of the same basis when the user enters none (COMFAR default, editable); the result names the rates used.
- MIRR: surpluses compounded at the reinvestment rate to the year after production ends (`P`), deficits discounted at the borrowing rate to the reference date (`N`), `MIRR = (P/N)^(1/n) − 1`; unique.

### 4.2 Cash-flow bases (XI.F)

- **Total capital invested:** `−(FA_SB + CA_SB − CL_SB) + Σ NCF_j + RV_{n+1}`; `NCF` is the financial-planning cash flow with all financing transactions removed (equity, disbursements, repayments, interest, fees, dividends). Residual value net of fixed assets, working capital and outstanding debt.
- **Total equity:** `−E_SB + Σ NCR_j + RV_{n+1}`; `NCR` removes equity contributions and adds back dividends.
- **Each shareholder class (joint venture):** `−E_SB,i + Σ(−ED + ER + PD + OD) + NW_i`.
- Starting-balance terms apply only to expansion/rehabilitation projects (§4.14).

### 4.3 Cost allocation (XI.G)

Indirect costs are allocated to products by a key: direct cost, direct factory cost, direct material, direct labour, sales, equal shares, or user percentages (constant). With cost-centre analysis, a centre's indirect costs go only to its products.

**Implementation (ST-34.03, `packages/financial-engine/src/model/operations.ts`).** A cost item with a product is a direct cost; one without is an indirect cost and is entered as amounts per period only (COMFAR's adjustments). Keys are computed per period from the direct costs of the products produced (direct material = raw materials + factory supplies; sales = net sales revenue + subsidy). The key is required for every indirect item that can go to more than one product; there is no default key (§3.1). User shares must add up to 100 %. A cost centre belongs to one of COMFAR's six standard groups (production, storage, environment, marketing, services, administration) and may list its products; an indirect item of a centre is shared among those products only, and a direct item may only be assigned to a centre of its product. Items without a centre are counted in the production group (X.C.3). The last product with a share takes the remainder, so the products always add up to the item exactly. When a key has no basis in a period (e.g. direct labour when no product has labour) the cost is shared equally and the result carries the warning `allocation.noBasis` (our rule; the manual is silent).

### 4.4 Depreciation (XI.H)

Methods: linear to zero, linear to scrap, accelerated (declining balance, switching to linear-to-scrap when that gives more), sum-of-years-digits. Life in years + months; first year may be partial (`m1` months); salvage = rate × initial book value; the remaining book value at the end is written off in the last year. Sale of an asset: proceeds above book value = extraordinary income, below = extraordinary loss; book value at the end of production returns as capital in the scrap year. (XI.I)

#### 4.4.1 Implementation rules (ST-33.06, `packages/financial-engine/src/depreciation.ts`)

The engine produces one schedule per asset and depreciation year (balance date to balance date). The start date, the grouping of assets bought before the start of production and the mapping to project periods belong to the model layer (ST-34.02). Inputs: method, IBV, life in months, salvage rate (0 ≤ SVR < 1), months of the first depreciation year `m1` (1–12) and, for the declining balance, its rate (0 < DR ≤ 1). All are required.

- **Exact total:** every method builds the cumulative charge per year and the last year closes at exactly `IBV − SV`, so a rounding residue in the 34th digit can neither add a tiny extra year nor push the book value below salvage.
- **Linear to zero and to scrap:** `base / L` a year (`base` = IBV or IBV − SV), `m1/12` of it in the first year, until IBV − SV is charged. This is the manual's `M = integral part of …` with `D_{M+1} = RBV_M − SV`, and it stays defined when `M < 1` (life shorter than the first year: everything is charged in year 1).
- **Declining balance, remaining life:** the manual prints `RL_j = L − j − m1/12 + 1`. Taken literally that is the life remaining **after** year j: with L = 5 and m1 = 12 the asset would be fully depreciated after four years and year 5 would divide by zero. The engine uses the life remaining at the **start** of year j, `RL_j = L − m1/12 − (j − 2)` (j ≥ 2), so the schedule spans L (OQ-39). Declining applies while `RBV × DR > (RBV − SV) / RL_j` (equal → linear, as VII.N says); after the switch the remainder is spread evenly over the remaining months, the last year possibly partial. A life that ends within the first depreciation year is written off in that year. **Base of the declining charge (manual inconsistent, OQ-39):** VII.N (table 9) describes the accelerated charge as a percentage of the prior book value _less salvage_ and the switch against the annual linear-to-scrap charge, while XI.H prints `DA_1 = IBV × DR × m1/12`, `DA_j = RBV_{j−1} × DR` (salvage not deducted) and the switch against `(RBV_{j−1} − SV)/RL_j`. The engine follows XI.H, the calculation rules; with a salvage value the two readings differ (e.g. IBV 1000, SVR 10 %, DR 30 %, m1 6: 150 by XI.H, 135 by VII.N). XI.H says the linear phase is "identical to linear to scrap"; the engine reads that as spreading the remaining `RBV − SV` evenly over the remaining life, which is what linear to scrap does from that point on.
- **Sum of years digits:** intermediate amounts per year of life as printed: with whole years `D_j = (L − j + 1)/SODL × (IBV − SV)`; with `m` extra months a first stub of `m` months, `D_1 = L/SODL × m/12 × (IBV − SV)`, then Y years on `IBV − D_1 − SV` with `SODL_adj = Y(Y+1)/2`; a life under one year is a single stub that takes all of IBV − SV (the printed `D_1` would charge only `m/12` of it and leave the rest undepreciated). The manual then shifts these onto depreciation years with a first year of `m1` months, but its printed shift formulas are not consistent: for `m1 > m` the two fractions of `DS_j` are swapped, and for `m1 ≤ m` the year-2 term divides the stub by 12 instead of `m`, so the total would not equal IBV − SV (OQ-39). The engine shifts by time: each depreciation year takes each life year's amount in proportion to the months they share. That equals the printed `DS_1` in both cases and the printed `DS_j` wherever the formulas are consistent, and it always depreciates exactly IBV − SV.
- **Revaluation:** with inflation and revaluation active, depreciation and book value of year k are multiplied by `FI_j / FI_i` (index of the year over the index of the acquisition year). The cumulative revaluation adjustment in net worth is `revalued book value + revalued depreciation charged so far − IBV`, so the balance sheet still balances.
- Capitalised interest is depreciated with the same methods (salvage 0).

#### 4.4.2 Depreciation in project periods (ST-34.02, `packages/financial-engine/src/model/asset-depreciation.ts`)

- **Start:** the user chooses, per item, the production period (start-up or production, never construction) on whose first day depreciation starts (VII.N «Starting at»). It is required, like the method; the editor may offer "start of production" through a visible "apply to all items" action, never a hidden pre-fill (§3.1).
- **Joint batch:** everything acquired before that day (flows on the last day of earlier periods, in construction or production) is depreciated together from it. Its first depreciation year runs to the next balance date: `m1` = months from the start to that balance date (partial when the start is not right after a balance date, e.g. a shortened first production year or a start in the second start-up quarter).
- **Later acquisitions:** each one (from the start period on, including replacements) starts at the beginning of the financial year after the year it was acquired in, with a full first year (VII.N).
- **Booking:** the charge of every depreciation year is booked in the period that contains its balance date (VII.N "charged at the balance date"); years after the last production year are not charged — the book value stays and returns as residual value. Book value per period = acquisitions so far − depreciation so far.
- **Values:** the value depreciated is the sum of the local-currency amounts, each converted at its own period's rate (VII.N). An item without depreciation conditions (e.g. land) is not depreciated; its book value is what was bought.
- The conditions (method, life, salvage rate, declining rate) are validated even when nothing is acquired yet; an invalid start period is refused with `investment.depreciationStart`.

### 4.5 Production costs (XI.J)

- At nominal capacity `PC`: standard cost `SC_j = Q × C_j`; fixed part `f × SC_j`; variable part `v × SC_j × S_j(P_j) / PC`; plus adjustments `A_j = Q_aj × C_aj` split by their own fixed/variable shares.
- Per unit of output: variable `Q × C_j × S_j(P_j)`; fixed is an absolute amount; adjustments as above.
- Fixed costs of short periods scale by `m/12`. Fixed costs are charged within the product's production interval even in no-sales periods (unless adjusted).

**Implementation (ST-34.03, `operations.ts`).**

- **Categories** (X.C.3 lines): raw materials, factory supplies, utilities, energy, spare parts (together: materials); labour, labour overheads, factory overheads (with materials: factory costs); administrative overheads (with factory costs: operating costs); leasing; direct marketing and marketing overheads. The schedule total is operating costs + leasing + marketing; depreciation and interest are added by the statements (ST-34.04). Maintenance, insurance and similar costs are the user's own items inside these categories.
- **Two quantities:** every schedule exists for the products produced (CPP, with `P_j`) and for the products sold (CPS, with `S_j`); fixed costs and adjustments are the same in both.
- **Prices** (`model/prices.ts`): an entered price is the price at the start of the horizon in the item's currency. The price of a period is the entered price × the price factor of the period's project year (§2.1; years end on balance dates and a period belongs to the year its last day falls in) × the period's exchange rate. Every sales line and cost item has its own escalation, so revenue, variable and fixed costs can each follow their own path. With inflation on, the inflation of every currency used and the escalation of every item are required ("0" is an explicit choice); without inflation an escalation is a real price path. The factor also applies to the fixed cost of the per-unit mode and to adjustments, which are entered at constant prices like everything else (our reading, OQ-39).
- **Adjustments** may be negative in production periods (e.g. to switch a fixed cost off in a shut-down period), but neither the fixed nor the variable cost of an item may become negative. In construction periods an adjustment is a purchase of initial stock and only material categories may have one.
- **Capacity utilisation** = production / (nominal capacity × m/12), within the product's production interval (the manual does not say how a short period is treated; OQ-39).

### 4.6 Sales and production programme (XI.L)

- Coefficient of turnover `c = 30 × m / days_coverage`.
- Finished-goods stock: required `RS_j = S_j / c`; brought forward `B_j = C_{j-1}` (starting balance first); `X = B − S`; carried `C = max(X, RS)`; production `P = S − B + C`. End stock is sold in period n+1.
- Sales ending before production ends, and user-defined production intervals, follow the special cases in XI.L (production reduced before the last sales year; sales outside the interval only from stock).

**Implementation (ST-34.03, `model/sales-programme.ts`, `operations.ts`).**

- **Sales lines:** a product has one or more sales lines, each for the local or the export market, with its own currency, price (one value or one per period, net of sales tax), escalation, sales-tax rate, subsidy rate and subsidy amount per period (all required; "0" is explicit). Volumes are entered as quantities per period or as a share of the nominal capacity (a period of `m` months sells `m/12` of the share; our extension for the production programme "in % of capacity"). Gross sales revenue = net + sales tax (information only); sales revenue = net + subsidy. The subsidy amount is converted at the period's rate and not escalated.
- **Programme:** as above, with `c = 30 × m / Mdc` per period. When the sales of the product's last production period are zero ("sales end early") the required stock is `min(S_j / c, Σ_{i>j} S_i)` and a period that sells less than its stock produces nothing, as printed. Outside a user-defined production interval sales must come from stock; selling more than the stock is refused with a message at the sales quantity of that period (COMFAR cuts the sales silently). The production interval defaults to the whole production phase (a structural default, not an economic one).
- The stock left at the end of the horizon is part of the working capital liquidated in the scrap year.

### 4.7 Net working capital (XI.K)

| Item                                                                        | Basis                                                            |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Materials (raw materials, factory supplies, utilities, energy, spare parts) | cost of products produced, materials part; units or value        |
| Work in progress                                                            | cost of products produced, factory cost                          |
| Finished products                                                           | cost of products sold, operating cost (from the programme above) |
| Accounts receivable                                                         | cost of products sold, operating + marketing                     |
| Cash-in-hand                                                                | cost of products produced, operating cost − materials            |
| Accounts payable                                                            | cost of products produced, materials … marketing                 |

- Requirement per item: `RV_j = B_j / c` (value) or `RS_j = S_j(P_j) × Q_j / c` (units, priced at a weighted unit value).
- Initial stocks and starting balances are consumed first: `X_j = WC_{j-1} − consumption_j`; `WC_j = max(X_j, RV_j)`.
- Increase in current assets = cash outflow; increase in current liabilities = inflow (short-term finance). Liquidated in the scrap period.
- Interest on short-term deposits: `IOS_j = CIH_j × s × r × m / 12`. (XI.O)

**Implementation (ST-34.03, `model/working-capital.ts`, `operations.ts`).**

- **Coverage** is required for every item (no default, §3.1), as days (COMFAR's Mdc) or as a share of the yearly basis (`days / 360`); 0 is an explicit "none". It is entered on the item it belongs to: stock and payables on each cost item (stock only for material categories), work in progress and finished products on each product, receivables on each sales line (market), cash once for local and once for foreign costs.
- **Value algorithm only.** Materials are valued by the total value algorithm; the units algorithm (which differs only when initial stock is consumed at changing prices, manual table 10) is not implemented.
- **Carry-over for stocks only (our reading, OQ-39).** The manual prints the recursion `X_j = WCV_{j−1} − B_j`, `WCV_j = max(X_j, RV_j)` for every item. For a material stock it means "what is left after this period's consumption" and is applied as printed. Applied to work in progress, receivables, cash and payables it would keep a balance for ever once the basis falls (receivables never collected and deposit interest still earned after sales stop), so these items equal their requirement `RV_j`. The two readings differ only when the previous value exceeds this period's basis plus requirement (coverage longer than a period, or a basis that drops sharply).
- **Bases:** materials and payables: the item's cost of products produced; work in progress: the product's factory cost (direct + allocated); receivables: the product's cost of products sold (operating + marketing), shared among its sales lines by quantity sold (our reading, OQ-39); cash: operating cost less materials of all items, separately for local and foreign origin.
- **Finished products** are the programme's stock at the operating cost per unit sold of the period, `C_j × CPS(OC)_j / S_j`. A period without sales produces nothing either, so the stock keeps its value (our reading; the manual defines the unit value only with sales, OQ-39). As printed, the unit value spreads the period's fixed costs over the units sold: in a period with few sales and a stock above the requirement the stock can be valued above the period's cost (kept as printed and covered by a test; OQ-39).
- **Initial stock** bought in construction periods is a current asset from the period it is bought in and is consumed first, as printed. Starting balances belong to expansion projects (ST-34.11).
- **Outputs:** every item per period, totals (inventory, receivables, cash, current assets, current liabilities, net working capital and its increase), the split by the origin of the underlying costs (an item's value follows the foreign share of its basis) and the value at the end of production, which is liquidated in the scrap year by the statements (ST-34.04). Short-term deposits are the entered share of the cash requirement, shown separately from cash in hand; their interest is per period as above.

### 4.8 Finance (XI.M)

- Equity: accumulated by class; preferred dividends use accumulated equity.
- Loans have a disbursement phase then a repayment phase:
  - annuity: payment `A = DB × IR(1+IR)^RP / ((1+IR)^RP − 1)`, `IR = I × LP/12`; interest `DB_{i-1} × IR`; principal `A − interest`; if `I = 0` use constant principal;
  - constant principal: `R = DB / RP`, interest on the opening balance of each period;
  - profile: user-entered disbursements/repayments at any time; interest by sub-intervals.
  - Disbursement phase ends one repayment period before the first repayment (annuity) or the day before it (constant principal); interest dates run back from the first repayment date; interest may be capitalised (adds to the balance and to pre-production expenditure, excluded from the total-capital investment base).
  - Fees: agency (per disbursement), guarantee (p.a. on the amount drawn and not yet repaid, with interest; the manual's wording is ambiguous, the engine uses the outstanding balance, see §4.8.1), commitment (p.a. on undrawn balance, with interest), other (on the total loan, at first disbursement).
- Short-term loans behave like profile loans.

#### 4.8.1 Implementation rules (ST-33.05, `packages/financial-engine/src/loans.ts`)

- **Dates** are day indices on the 30/360 calendar: days from the start of the horizon to the end of the date, with 30-day months (day 360 = end of year 1, day 361 = first day of year 2). Interest for `n` days is `balance × rate × n / 360`, which is COMFAR's `m/12 + d/360`. The model layer converts calendar dates.
- **Interest by sub-intervals:** interest accrues between consecutive events (disbursement, repayment, rate change, due date). A rate entered from day X applies from day X on. Interest accrued up to a due date is paid there, or, in the disbursement phase and up to the "capitalise until" due date, capitalised by the entered share (0–100 %, required; the rest is paid). This covers interest during construction and grace periods, capitalised or paid. Capitalisation must end before the first repayment.
- **Annuity and constant principal:** due dates run back from the first repayment in steps of the repayment period (monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or yearly) and forward for the repayments. The disbursement phase ends one repayment period before the first repayment (annuity) or the day before it (constant principal); later disbursements are refused. `DB_n` is the balance at the start of repayment. Constant principal repays `DB_n / RP`; the annuity instalment is `A = B × IR(1+IR)^n / ((1+IR)^n − 1)` on the remaining balance and number of instalments, with `IR` the interval's effective rate, so it stays constant at a constant rate and follows a rate change otherwise; at `IR = 0` the constant principal rule applies. The last repayment is the remaining balance, so the loan always closes at exactly 0.
- **First repayment date (owner decision, COMFAR default):** one repayment period after the last disbursement or after the end of construction, whichever is later, moved to the next month end. The "last disbursement" is the last flow of the disbursement phase of either sign, so repayments entered there stay inside it. It is recomputed from the current flows until the user enters a date, and reported in `defaultsUsed`. Error parameters such as the end of the disbursement phase are day indices; the model layer shows them as dates.
- **Profile loans:** disbursements and repayments on any day (on one day, disbursements before repayments); interest due every repayment period from the entered month end, until the end of the horizon; interest is accrued up to the horizon end and rates entered after it are ignored. A balance left at the end of the horizon, or interest accrued after the last due date, gives a warning. Repaying more than the balance is refused.
- **Fees:** agency on each disbursement (paid with it); guarantee per year on the outstanding balance and commitment per year on the undisbursed part of the total disbursements (accrued from the first disbursement, paid with interest); other on the total disbursements, at the first disbursement. Fees are financial costs, never capitalised.
- **Periods:** a schedule is summed into project periods (flows on a period's last day belong to it) as opening balance, disbursement, repayment, capitalised interest, interest paid, fees and closing balance. Several loans in one currency add up period by period; foreign-currency loans are restated with `foreignLoanToLocal` first (§2.1).

#### 4.8.2 Sources of finance per period (ST-34.02, `packages/financial-engine/src/model/financing.ts`)

- **Equity:** contributions per class (ordinary, preference, joint-venture partner, subsidy/grant), each in its own currency and origin, converted at the period's rate; totals per class, per origin and overall.
- **Loans:** each loan is scheduled in its own currency (§4.8.1) with the horizon's end of construction (for COMFAR's default first repayment date) and end of horizon, summed into the horizon's periods and, for a foreign currency, restated with the period's rates. Warnings and the defaults used carry the loan's key.
- **Pre-production interest:** capitalised interest in any period, plus interest and fees paid in construction periods, of each loan (VII.R: the balance sheet's pre-production line holds the interest capitalised up to the "capitalise until" date and all interest and fees up to the end of construction). It is excluded from the total investment cost (X.C.2) and depreciated with the loan's own conditions, salvage 0 (VII.R «Depreciation of interest»; start in production, mapped as in §4.4.2), starting no earlier than the month after the last capitalisation day (`financing.interestDepreciationStart`; only when a share above 0 is capitalised). Without conditions it stays on the books. Interest and fees are classified by the day they are paid, so interest accrued in construction but due at the first due date in production is a production cost. One set of conditions covers the loan's interest and fees; COMFAR offers separate conditions for fees (OQ-39). Schedule X-2 describes the interest line as "interest payable and capitalized during the disbursement phase and period of grace", which differs when the grace period runs into production; the balance-sheet rule is used (OQ-39).
- **Sources:** equity paid in plus loan disbursements, per period. Balancing sources against uses, the cash deficit and its automatic coverage belong to the cash flow for financial planning (ST-34.04).

### 4.9 Cash deficit (XI.N)

Default as in COMFAR (owner decision, 2026-10-02): automatic local equity covers a deficit in a construction period (including interest payable) and an interest-free automatic overdraft covers a cumulative deficit in a production period, repaid from the earliest surpluses. Unlike COMFAR, nothing is hidden: the automatic lines are labelled in every schedule and the result warns that the plan is under-financed in the affected periods. The user may switch automatic coverage off; the deficit then shows as negative cash.

### 4.10 Income tax (XI.P) and allowances (XI.R)

- Graduated brackets: each slice taxed at its own rate (worked example in the manual becomes a unit test).
- Tax holiday: no tax for the first N production years.
- Losses carried forward up to N years, used in the earliest possible year (worked example becomes a unit test).
- Investment allowance reduces taxable profit only; depreciation allowance reduces taxable profit and the book value of fixed assets.

### 4.11 Dividends (XI.Q)

- Retained profit = `rr %` of positive net profit; no dividends on a loss.
- Preferred dividends = accumulated equity × rate + absolute amount; if profit available is short, joint-venture partners are served first (three cases with reduction factors).
- Ordinary dividends share the remainder by percentage; a repatriated share per equity class goes to the foreign cash flow.

### 4.12 Investment (X.C.1–2, VII.N; ST-34.02, `packages/financial-engine/src/model/investment.ts`)

- **Items:** fixed-investment groups as in COMFAR — land, site preparation and development, buildings and civil works, plant machinery and equipment, auxiliary and service facilities, incorporated fixed assets, contingencies, other — and pre-production expenditures. Each item has a currency, a local/foreign origin (independent of the currency, COMFAR "designation as a local or foreign item"), an acquisition amount per project period and optional depreciation conditions (§4.4.2).
- **Conversion and current prices:** amounts are entered at the prices of the start of the horizon and follow the inflation of their currency and the item's own escalation like every other price (§2.1, §4.5; ST-34.04), then are converted at the exchange rate of their own period (local units per foreign unit; a missing or non-positive rate is refused). With inflation on, the escalation of every item is required ("0" is explicit); at constant prices the amounts are taken as entered unless the item has an escalation, which is then a real price path (as in §4.5).
- **Outputs per period (local currency):** every item, every group (all groups listed), total fixed investment with its foreign/local split, pre-production expenditures net of interest with their split, total investment (fixed + pre-production net of interest; the interest of construction comes from §4.8.2 and the increase in net working capital from ST-34.03), and depreciation and book value of fixed assets, pre-production expenditures and both.

### 4.13 Financial statements (X.C.6–7; ST-34.04, `packages/financial-engine/src/model/statements.ts`)

`financialStatements` puts the investment, financing and operations schedules together, per project period and in local currency. Helpers: `model/tax.ts` (`incomeTax`, `graduatedTax`) and `model/dividends.ts` (`distributeProfit`).

- **Yearly amounts.** Tax and dividends are computed on the profit of each financial year of production (the periods up to and including the one that holds the balance date) and booked in the period of the balance date, like depreciation. With yearly periods this is the period itself; in a start-up phase the earlier periods of a year show no tax.
- **Net income statement.** Sales revenue; variable and fixed costs of the products **sold**; depreciation (fixed assets, pre-production expenditures and pre-production interest); interest on short-term deposits; financial costs = interest and fees paid in production periods (interest that is capitalised or paid in construction is an asset, §4.8.2); extraordinary income or loss; depreciation allowance; gross profit; investment allowance; deductible loss; taxable profit; tax; net profit; dividends; retained profit. The manual's sub-lines of variable and fixed costs (material, personnel, marketing, other) are not produced yet: the schedules carry fixed and variable totals only. Depreciation is charged in full; the manual's "adjusted for finished products on stock" is not applied to it (OQ-39).
- **Income tax (§4.10).** Brackets by lower limit (the first is 0) with a rate per bracket and production year; the manual's bracket example (tax 3 080 on 7 500) and its loss example (three years carry-forward) are unit tests. The loss of a year is its profit after allowances when negative; it is used by the oldest loss first and expires after the entered number of years. Losses are also used up by the profits of holiday years, since the deduction belongs to the taxable profit line and the holiday to the tax (our reading, OQ-39). Bracket limits are amounts in local currency and are not inflated. Absolute tax adjustments per period (VII.S) are not implemented.
- **Allowances (§4.10).** Per production period, in local currency. The investment allowance reduces the taxable profit only; the depreciation allowance reduces the gross profit and the book value of fixed assets, and what it has written off is not depreciated again: the ordinary charge of a period is reduced as far as the book value of fixed investment and pre-production expenditures (all items together; COMFAR's allowance is one amount, not tied to an asset) would otherwise fall below the allowances granted, and the balance sheet shows the allowances less the charges they replaced. An allowance that the remaining book value cannot cover, also after a sale of assets, is refused at the period it was entered in. Because the allowance is not tied to an asset, after a sale it stays against the remaining assets (also non-depreciable ones such as land) and so reduces the residual value (our rule; the manual only says the allowance reduces the book value, OQ-39).
- **Sale of assets (XI.I).** A fixed-investment or pre-production item may be sold on the last day of a production period that ends on a balance date, for a price in local currency. The proceeds are other income of the cash flow; proceeds less book value are extraordinary income or loss; the item is not depreciated afterwards and leaves the books. Acquisitions of the item after the sale are refused. Without a sale the book value returns as residual value.
- **Dividends (§4.11).** Per equity contribution (not subsidies): preferred rate on the accumulated paid-in equity plus an absolute amount, share of the remaining profit and repatriated share, per production year. The three cases of XI.Q are tests; in case 3 the reduction factor is `DP / TPD(k)` (the printed fraction is garbled). The shares of the remaining profit must add up to 100 % in every year in which less than 100 % is retained. Dividends are paid from the year's net profit even when losses of earlier years are still on the books, as printed. Equity refunds and the distribution of net worth to partners are in §4.15.
- **Cash flow for financial planning.** Inflows: equity (with subsidies), long-term loans (disbursements and capitalised interest), increase of accounts payable (a decrease is negative; COMFAR shows it under loan repayments), sales revenue, interest on deposits, proceeds of assets sold. Outflows: fixed investment, pre-production expenditures net of interest, increase of current assets, operating, leasing and marketing costs of the products sold, tax, financial costs (interest paid, capitalised interest, fees), loan repayments, dividends. `surplus` and `cumulativeSurplus` are COMFAR's lines, before any automatic coverage. The foreign/local split of this schedule is not produced yet.
- **Cash deficit (§4.9).** When the cumulative balance turns negative, a construction period gets automatic equity and a production period draws on an interest-free automatic overdraft, which is repaid from the first surpluses. Both are separate lines of the cash flow (`automaticEquity`, `automaticOverdraft`) and of the balance sheet, the result carries the warning `cash.underFinanced` with the periods, and `defaultsUsed` lists `cash.autoCoverage`. With the coverage switched off the cash balance is negative and the warning is `cash.deficit`. The coverage never changes the two discounted cash flows.
- **Balance sheet.** Assets: cash surplus (the cash balance after coverage), the working-capital items, fixed assets (fixed investment, pre-production expenditures and pre-production interest at book value, less depreciation allowances), accumulated losses and exchange losses. Liabilities: accounts payable, automatic overdraft, long-term debt, equity by class, automatic equity, reserves (cumulative retained profit) and exchange gains. Both sides are equal in every period (tests; to the engine's 34 digits when quotients are involved). Net worth = equity + cumulative retained profit − exchange losses (+ gains), i.e. assets less debts to third parties; the manual's net worth leaves the exchange lines out (OQ-39). The exchange adjustment of foreign loans is shown as the balance sheet's own lines, as X.C.6 lists them; the footnote of the net income statement, which puts it into the financial costs, is not followed (OQ-39).
- **Discounted cash flows (§4.2).** Total capital: sales revenue, deposit interest and proceeds of assets, less fixed investment, pre-production expenditures, increase of net working capital, operating, leasing and marketing costs and the tax of the income statement. Equity: surplus plus dividends, less equity paid in net of subsidies. Residual value: book value of fixed investment and pre-production expenditures plus net working capital for the total capital (pre-production interest is not part of this basis), and with the book value of pre-production interest and less the outstanding long-term debt for the equity. It returns in an extra column for the year after production (COMFAR default, `assets.residualValuePeriod`) or, when the user chooses so, on the last day of production; the extra year is discounted at the rate of the last period. Both bases have their own discount rate (required) and give NPV, IRR, MIRR, normal and dynamic payback; the total capital also the NPV ratio and profitability index with `I_j = FI_j + PPN_j + IWC_j` (0 in the extra year).
- **Break-even** of every production period from the income statement (fixed costs include depreciation; financial costs as above); the selected year — the reference year unless the user chooses another (`breakEven.period`) — is analysed as a whole financial year, because yearly amounts such as depreciation are booked in one period of the year. **Debt-service coverage** uses the surplus before automatic coverage. **Ratios** per period: net profit to sales, to equity capital and to net worth, long-term debt to net worth and current ratio; a ratio that cannot be computed is null, not zero.

### 4.14 Starting balances of an existing enterprise (VII.T, XI.F, XI.K–M; ST-34.11, `packages/financial-engine/src/model/starting-balances.ts`)

An expansion or rehabilitation project carries `startingBalances` in its input; a new project has none. Every balance is entered by the user ("0" and an empty list are explicit); the engine has no default. Amounts are in local currency, as in COMFAR's STARTING BALANCE window, except the balance of a loan, which is in the loan's currency. The effective date is the day before the first project period.

- **Fixed assets:** a book value per fixed-investment or pre-production item. It is not a cash outflow; it is depreciated with the item's own conditions together with everything acquired before the start of depreciation (VII.N: the effective date of investment for starting balances is the first day of the project), and it is part of the book value and so of the residual value.
- **Stocks:** a value per material cost item, consumed first like initial stock (§4.7); a value of work in progress per product; quantity and price of finished products per product, which is the stock brought forward into the first period (`C_0 = B_1 = SB`, §4.6) and is valued at its price until a period with sales revalues it.
- **Receivables and payables** are not part of the requirement algorithm (table 18, footnote): they are collected and paid on the entered day, counted from the start of the project on the 30/360 calendar, in the period that contains that day (day 0: the first period). Until then they are part of the receivables and current liabilities of the working capital; a day beyond the horizon leaves them outstanding and they are liquidated with the working capital.
- **Cash-in-hand** is the value of the item before production and falls to its requirement in production; **short-term deposits** are added to the deposits made from the cash requirement and stay to the end (XI.O); they earn no interest, since the printed formula is on the cash-in-hand of the period (OQ-39). The cash-in-hand of a production period is the requirement less all deposits, those of the starting balance included (VII.Q), and never below zero. The **cash surplus** is the opening balance of the cash flow for financial planning.
- **Loans:** the outstanding balance of a loan, which then needs no disbursement (XI.M). Interest accrues from day 1, so a rate from day 1 is required; a foreign loan is converted at the first period's rate.
- **Equity:** an amount per contribution. It counts as equity held for preferred dividends, the balance sheet and the ratios.
- **Balancing amount:** assets less liabilities and equity are carried into the project as reserves (retained profit), or as accumulated losses when negative, so the balance sheet balances from the first day (X.C.6). The statements return this opening balance sheet as `startingBalance`.
- **Increases** of current assets, payables and net working capital in the first period are measured from the starting balance.
- **Discounted cash flows (§4.2):** the total capital is charged with fixed assets plus current assets (cash surplus included, as printed; OQ-39) less current liabilities, the equity with the starting equity without subsidies, on the day before the first period: a column of no length in front of the series (`TimedSeries` allows a first length of 0). With the reference at the end of the first year that amount is compounded forward one year. The result reports it as `startingBalance` with its present value; `net` stays one value per period and the cumulative lines start from the charge. For the NPV ratio the charge of the total capital counts as investment on that day: the current assets it holds return later as negative increases of working capital, so leaving it out would net the investment down (our rule, OQ-39).
- **Not offered:** a starting stock of materials by quantity and price (COMFAR's units algorithm, §4.7), and the foreign-currency component of the balances (economic analysis). A finished stock entered at a price other than the unit cost of its first sales period changes the cash balance but not the profit: the stock is revalued like any stock carried over (§4.7), and the difference is taken from the reserves of the starting balance (OQ-39).
- A balance of an item that does not exist, of a cost item that is not a material, a negative amount or a day that is not a whole number from 0 to 36 000 is refused at its field (`startingBalances.…`).

### 4.15 Shareholders and joint-venture partners (VII.L, VII.R, XI.F, X.C.6; ST-34.12, `model/financing.ts`, `model/statements.ts`)

- **Partners** are equity contributions of the class joint-venture partner, each with a local or foreign origin (VII.L). They come first when preferred dividends must be reduced (§4.11).
- **Refund of equity (VII.R.1):** a contribution may carry amounts paid out again per period, as positive amounts in its own currency. A refund is a cash outflow of the cash flow for financial planning (`equityRefunds`) and reduces the equity of the balance sheet and the equity on which preferred dividends are paid. The equity held never turns negative (counted in the contribution's currency; a starting balance is converted at the first period's rate); subsidies and grants are not refunded. For the equity as a whole a refund is neutral, like a dividend: the discounted cash flow of the equity adds it back to the surplus (X.C.6: the net equity contribution, equity paid less refunds, is the outflow).
- **Share of the net worth:** each shareholder may carry the share of the net worth it receives after the last production period (VII.R.4). It is entered for every shareholder or for none and adds up to 100 %; it has no default.
- **Cash flow of a shareholder (XI.F):** `−E_SB,i + Σ(−ED + ER + PD + OD) + NW_i` — the starting equity on the day before the project (§4.14), equity paid in, refunds, preferred and ordinary dividends per period, and the shareholder's part of the net worth. It is produced for every shareholder with dividend conditions when the shares of the net worth are entered (`statements.shareholders`), with NPV, IRR, MIRR, payback and dynamic payback by the code of §4.13; the warnings of a flow stay with it and carry the shareholder's name.
- **Our rules (OQ-39):** the net worth distributed is that of the last balance sheet (equity plus reserves, the accumulated cash included), returned when residual values return (the year after production, or its last day); the flows are discounted at the discount rate of the equity, as COMFAR's schedule has one rate.
- **Not offered:** the foreign and local cash flows of repatriated dividends (economic analysis), and the joint-venture basis of the incremental analysis (§7.2).

## 5. Outputs (X.C), one schedule each

1. **Summary sheet** – key figures from the schedules below.
2. **Investment costs** – fixed investment, pre-production expenditures (net of capitalised interest), net working capital requirements by item, total investment; by period and currency (foreign/local).
3. **Production costs** – total costs of products (direct/indirect, fixed/variable, by category), cost allocation by cost centre and product.
4. **Production and sales programme** – quantities, stock, gross sales revenue, sales tax, subsidy, net revenue; local/export.
5. **Sources of finance** – financial flow (equity classes, loans, short-term finance), total debt and debt service, per loan.
6. **Business results**:
   - Cash flow for financial planning (inflows, outflows, surplus/deficit, cumulative cash balance; total, foreign, local);
   - Discounted cash flow, total capital invested (net cash flow, cumulative, NPV, IRR, MIRR, normal and dynamic payback);
   - Discounted cash flow, total equity (and per shareholder class);
   - Net income statement (sales; variable costs by material/personnel/marketing/other; variable margin; fixed costs incl. depreciation; operational margin; interest on deposits; financial costs; gross profit from operations; extraordinary items; allowances; deductible loss; taxable profit; tax; net profit; dividends; retained profit) and ratios (net profit to equity, to net worth, margins);
   - Projected balance sheet (current assets by item, fixed assets net of depreciation, accumulated losses; liabilities, debt, equity, reserves, revaluation and exchange adjustments) — must balance (test).
7. **Evaluation and ratios** – financial ratios (long-term debt to net worth, current ratio, cash flow to debt, debtors to creditors, debt-service coverage) and efficiency ratios; break-even analysis.

**Export (owner requirement, 2026-10-03; ST-34.09).** Every schedule of a calculation run must be downloadable as xlsx, PDF and standalone HTML, with the same tables in COMFAR's order and structure, built from one data source so the three formats show the same figures, right-to-left with Persian digits, and with the COMFAR defaults used and the engine's warnings. §5.2 describes how. Lines the engine does not produce yet (§4.13) are named in the report and are added to it as soon as the engine produces them.

### 5.1 Indicator definitions (manual X.C.6–7; implemented in ST-33.04)

- **Normal payback period:** duration until, and date of, the first period in which the cumulative net cash flow becomes positive.
- **Dynamic payback period:** the same on the cumulative net present value (discounted at the project discount rate).
- **NPV ratio (NPVR):** `NPV / PVI`, where `PVI` is the present value of total investment per period `I_j = FI_j + PPN_j + IWC_j` (fixed investment + pre-production expenditures + increment of net working capital), over the project periods plus the salvage period.
- **Break-even (per production period, total and per product when cost allocation is active):**
  - variable margin = sales revenue − variable costs; variable margin ratio = margin / revenue;
  - including costs of finance: break-even sales value = (fixed costs excluding interest + interest) / variable margin ratio;
  - excluding costs of finance: break-even sales value = fixed costs excluding interest / variable margin ratio;
  - break-even ratio (% of capacity utilisation) = break-even sales value / sales revenue × 100;
  - fixed-cost coverage ratio = variable margin / (fixed costs [+ financial costs]).
- **Debt-service coverage:** `CF_j / DS_j`, where `CF_j` = surplus of the financial-planning cash flow + repayment + interest + other financial costs, and `DS_j` = repayment + interest + other financial costs (long-term loans).

A ratio that cannot be computed is shown as "not calculable" with a reason, **not** as zero (deviation from COMFAR, which prints zero).

**Implementation rules (ST-33.04, `packages/financial-engine/src/indicators.ts`).** Where the manual leaves a detail open, the engine does the following; each point is covered by a unit test.

- **Payback, both kinds:** time is measured in months from the start of the planning horizon. A salvage value arrives on the last day of the horizon: if the operating flows alone recover within the last period, the interpolation uses them only; if only the salvage completes the payback, the duration is the end of the horizon. The result gives COMFAR's payback period (the first period whose cumulative amount is positive) and its date (end of that period), plus an interpolated duration: the point inside that period where the cumulative amount crosses zero, assuming the period's amount accrues evenly. The interpolation is our extension (COMFAR books flows at period end); reports label it.
- The cumulative amount must have been negative before it turns positive; a series that is never negative has no payback (`payback.noInvestment`, or `dynamicPayback.noInvestment` for the dynamic payback). A cumulative of exactly zero is not yet recovered. If the cumulative falls to zero or below again later (e.g. a replacement investment), the first payback is kept, as in COMFAR, and a warning names the period.
- **Dynamic payback** uses the project discount rate (or rate path) and reports with its own warning codes (`dynamicPayback.…`), so that it can be told apart from the normal payback. The discount reference date scales every present value by the same positive constant, so it cannot move the payback and is not an input.
- **NPVR:** the caller supplies the cash-flow series of the total capital invested and the investment per period `I_j = FI_j + PPN_j + IWC_j` (positive amounts), over the project periods plus the salvage period. PVI ≤ 0 makes the ratio "not calculable". The ratio does not depend on the reference date; NPV and PVI do, and the result reports the default.
- **Profitability index** (not a COMFAR output) = PV of the net cash flow before investment / PVI = `1 + NPVR`. **Benefit-cost ratio** (not a COMFAR output) = PV of benefits / PV of costs, discounted like the NPV.
- **Break-even, all products:** inputs per production period are sales revenue, variable costs, fixed costs excluding interest and interest (all from the schedules, adjusted for finished-goods stock). Both variants (including and excluding costs of finance) are returned. With planned sales per product the mix is held constant, so every product breaks even at the same share of its planned sales (break-even ratio × planned volume and revenue). The product revenues must add up to the period's sales revenue.
- **Break-even, each product** (COMFAR, with cost allocation): constant-price analysis (break-even value, volume at the average unit price, ratio to planned volume) and constant-volume analysis (break-even price = (fixed + variable costs) / planned volume, ratio to the average price). The caller decides which fixed costs the product must cover (COMFAR: excluding interest).
- **DSCR** per period as above; periods without debt service have no ratio (not zero), and the result names the period with the lowest ratio.
- **LLCR** (project-finance practice, not a COMFAR output): for every period with long-term debt outstanding at its start, the present value at that date of the cash available for debt service (`CF_j` above) up to the last period with debt outstanding, divided by the opening debt. The discount rate is entered by the user (usually the loans' interest rate); there is no default.
- **WACC** (helper for choosing a discount rate, never applied automatically): `Σ w_i × k_i` over equity and debt sources, debt after tax `k × (1 − t)`; tax rate in [0, 1).
- Ratios are decimal fractions ("0.625" = 62.5 %). Every warning and input-error code has a Persian message in `src/messages.ts`; a test fails when a new code has none.

### 5.2 Export of a run (ST-34.09, ADR-0011; `packages/financial-report`, `apps/api/src/modules/financial-model`)

- **One document, three files.** `runReport` turns a stored run into a report document; the xlsx, the PDF and the HTML are written from that document and from nothing else, and the result pages read the same table definitions. A number is rounded once, in the document: amounts in the display unit (whole numbers in single units, one decimal otherwise), percentages and ratios with two decimals (the NPV ratio and the lowest debt-service coverage of the summary with three, as on the page), quantities with two. The xlsx stores that rounded figure as a number whose format shows the same decimals; a value that is too long for a spreadsheet number (more than 15 significant digits or 10 decimals) is written as text, digit for digit.
- **Parts, in COMFAR's order:** summary and indicators (facts of the run — number, time, version of the inputs, engine version, input hash, approval — indicators of both bases with the warning that belongs to each, break-even and lowest debt-service coverage, the other warnings, the COMFAR defaults used, how to read the report and which lines are not produced yet); assumptions and inputs; investment costs with depreciation, book values and net working capital; production costs (produced, sold, by cost centre and product); production and sales programme of every product and the sales of all; sources of finance with every loan; cash flow for financial planning; discounted cash flows; net income statement; balance sheet; ratios, break-even and debt service.
- **Inputs are shown as entered:** amounts in the currency of their item and never in the display unit, rates and shares in percent, one value per column where the user entered one value for all. A convention the user left open reads «وارد نشده (پیش‌فرض COMFAR)»; the notes on source and as-of date are listed.
- **Totals are the engine's lines.** A total the engine does not compute (for instance fixed investment plus working capital) is not printed, rather than summed by the report.
- **A stored run is read, never recalculated.** A part whose stored data has another shape (an older engine) shows a plain message; the other parts stay. Among the inputs, a table whose series does not fit the horizon (a rate of a currency no item uses is never checked by the engine) says so alone. A line that is not one value per column is refused, not padded.
- **HTML:** one file without script or outside request, the font (Vazirmatn, SIL OFL) embedded, a content-security policy in the file and on the response, every text escaped. **xlsx:** one right-to-left sheet per part, Persian digits through the number format, texts as inline strings (never formulas) and marked as text when they start like one. **PDF:** A4 landscape with the font embedded; a table wider than the page continues in further parts with the same lines, a longer one repeats its heading; names and codes inside Persian text are kept together by Unicode isolates.
- **API:** `GET /financial-models/:id/runs/:runId/export?format=xlsx|pdf|html&unit=1|1000|1000000|1000000000` returns the file. Access is that of the model (owner, assigned expert, staff; 404 otherwise). The file is written in a worker thread (one at a time per API process, at most four in the queue with the one being written, 30 seconds of work and 512 MB each); a user has one file in the making at a time and at most 10 per minute; every download is in the audit log (`calculation_run.exported`).
- **Open:** approvals are shown with their time only (the names come with the deliverable of ST-35.14); the report has no charts; scenario and sensitivity results are not exported because they are not stored.

## 6. Economic analysis (VIII, XII, X.D) — EPIC-37

All values in a user-chosen numeraire; every parameter user-entered.

- **Value added:** `GDVA = (O + OI) − M`; `NDVA = GDVA − I`; `NNVA = NDVA − R` (repatriated wages, profits, interest, others); distribution to wages, profits/interest, government (taxes net of subsidies) and others; present values.
- **Net foreign-exchange effect:** foreign inflows minus outflows over the horizon, and its present value.
- **Employment:** direct employment by group from the labour inputs; indirect employment user-entered; investment per job.
- **Cost-benefit:** items classified and converted from financial to economic value (remove taxes/duties/subsidies, adjust market prices, foreign-exchange adjustment via shadow exchange rate, indirect effects), conversion to the numeraire, economic NPV and IRR at the economic discount rate.

Details of chapter XII are read again when ST-37.01–37.04 start.

## 7. Sensitivity and incremental analysis (XIII, XIV)

- **Global change of input data:** scale groups of inputs (e.g. all sales prices −10 %) and recompute.
- **Goal seek:** the value of one input that yields a target IRR or NPV.
- **Charts:** indicator against variations of chosen inputs (our tornado and spider charts).
- **Incremental analysis:** difference between "with project" and "without project" cases of an existing enterprise, indicators on the difference.

### 7.1 Implementation (ST-34.05, `packages/financial-engine/src/model/project.ts`, `sensitivity.ts`)

- **One model run.** `projectModel` takes the inputs the user entered (horizon, currencies and rates, investment, financing, operations, and the options of the statements) and runs every schedule; an input error carries the section it is in (`operations.costs[2]…`). Every analysis below changes these inputs and runs the whole model again, as COMFAR's Sensitivity feature does — so tax, working capital, interest and cash deficits follow the change. COMFAR's quicker "parametric analysis" of charts, which leaves working capital and financing unchanged, is not offered.
- **Changes** (`applyChanges`) are relative to the original value and constant over the horizon, as in COMFAR ("-0.1" = 10 % less; not below −100 %). Targets, each for one item or for all items of its kind:
  - sales (a product, a sales line): quantity (quantities or capacity shares) and price;
  - production costs (an item or a category): quantity (standard quantity and adjustment quantities) and price (standard price, the fixed cost of the per-unit mode, adjustment prices);
  - fixed investment (an item or a group; pre-production expenditures are not part of it): the amounts, by the quantity and the price change together;
  - our extensions, as rates: exchange rates (a currency), inflation (a currency) and the two discount rates; a 10 % change turns a rate of 20 % into 22 %.
    A target that matches nothing is refused. Changes of the same item multiply. The subsidy amount of a sales line is an absolute amount and follows neither change.
- **Scenarios** (`scenarioAnalysis`): the base case and any number of named sets of changes (optimistic, pessimistic, the user's own), each with NPV, IRR, MIRR, normal and dynamic payback of both bases, the NPV ratio and its own warnings. The engine ships no scenario: the percentages are the user's.
- **Sensitivity** (`sensitivityAnalysis`): every variable (a target and a dimension) is changed by every step the user entered while the others keep their value. The result holds every point (for spider charts) and, for each basis, tornado bars — NPV at the lowest and the highest step (the base case is one end when all steps lie on one side) and their distance — sorted by that distance. Every point lists the warnings its run adds to the base case, e.g. an under-financed plan.
- **Critical values** (`criticalValues`): the change of a variable at which the NPV of the chosen basis is zero, searched below and above the original value within the range the user entered. A variable without one is returned empty with the warning `sensitivity.noCriticalValue`. The search assumes one sign change on each side of the original value (it looks at the ends of the range, then bisects); a second crossing inside one side would not be found.
- **Goal seek** (`goalSeek`): COMFAR's desired NPV or IRR. The variables are used in the order given, each up to its maximal change (with its sign); a variable that does not reach the target stays at its limit and the next one continues. COMFAR moves in steps of one percent; the engine finds the change at which the target is met (bisection to 10⁻⁸ of the change), so the answer is not rounded to whole percents. The result lists the change and the value achieved after each variable; a target out of reach, a change in the wrong direction (the variable is still taken to its limit, as COMFAR does when the user continues) and an indicator that cannot be calculated or jumps over the target on the way (no unique IRR) are warnings. The result carries the defaults and warnings of the base case and the warnings that the plan with the changes found adds.

### 7.2 Incremental analysis (ST-34.11, `packages/financial-engine/src/model/incremental.ts`)

COMFAR's rule (XIV, VII.T): the effect of a project on an existing enterprise is the difference between two cases with the same planning horizon — the enterprise with the project and the enterprise without the new investment.

- `incrementalAnalysis` takes the results of two calculated cases (their horizon and statements, as a stored run holds them) and the discounting of the difference. It returns the cash flow for financial planning of the difference, line by line, and the discounted cash flows of the total capital and of the equity of the difference with their own NPV, IRR, MIRR, payback and dynamic payback, computed by the same code as the statements (`model/discounted-flow.ts`).
- The two cases must have the same periods (length and calendar end of each) and return residual values at the same time; otherwise the analysis is refused (`incremental.horizonMismatch`).
- Starting balances the cases share cancel: the difference of the charges of §4.14 is charged, usually zero, and the result is the project in isolation.
- The engine has no discount rate of its own: the caller passes one. The page uses the rates, the reference date and the MIRR rates of the case with the project (our rule; COMFAR does not say which project's rates apply, OQ-39).
- **Page (`apps/web/src/components/models/results/incremental.tsx`):** on the page of a run, the user chooses another stored run — of the same model or of another model they may read — as the case without the project. The difference is calculated in the browser from the two stored results and is never stored; another choice removes the result. Tables come from `@roshd/financial-report/tables` (`incrementalFlowTable`, `incrementalCashFlowTable`). The two runs must have the same local currency. The export of an incremental analysis and the joint-venture basis (XIV lists it) are not offered yet.

## 8. Test plan for the engine

- Every formula above has unit tests; the manual's worked examples (graduated tax, loss carry-forward, working-capital tables) become fixtures.
- Property tests: NPV at each IRR ≈ 0; annuity and constant-principal schedules repay exactly the principal; the balance sheet balances every period; cumulative cash in the financial-planning cash flow equals the balance-sheet cash.
- Acceptance (ST-36.01): a reference study prepared in COMFAR, re-entered in our editor, must reproduce its schedules within a documented tolerance; differences explained (e.g. our explicit cash-deficit handling).
