# ADR-0010: Feasibility platform

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-02 (accepted 2026-10-06 with ST-35.01)

## Context

Today a feasibility request is a `ServiceRequest` of type `FEASIBILITY`: a form, a tracking code and a staff status. Phase 1 also defined the workflow contract, a pure state machine in `apps/api/src/modules/feasibility/domain/feasibility-status.ts` (`DRAFT → SUBMITTED → … → DELIVERED → ARCHIVED`, with actor rules). Phase 3 turns this into a platform where the applicant fills a questionnaire and uploads documents, staff estimate and contract the work, experts build the study with the financial engine (ADR-0009), and the client receives a versioned report.

Open business points: pricing (OQ-01), contract and staged payment (OQ-02), document confidentiality (OQ-07). Answered by the owner on 2026-10-02: the report follows the UNIDO methodology with COMFAR financials (OQ-35); questionnaires and document lists are prepared by the feasibility officer and the admin, and the applicant adds project-specific items (OQ-36); the report is approved first by the feasibility officer, then by the site admin (OQ-37).

## Decision

1. **A `feasibility` module** owns `FeasibilityProject`, `FeasibilityStatusEvent`, `QuestionnaireTemplate` (versioned), `QuestionnaireResponse`, `ProjectDocument` links, `ExpertAssignment`, `ReviewComment`, `CostEstimate` and `Deliverable`. It uses `files`, `notifications`, `audit` and `financial-model` only through their exported services.
2. **Roles.** A new role, feasibility officer (`feasibility_officer`), holds `feasibility:manage` and `feasibility:approve-report`; admins and super admins hold `feasibility:final-approve`.
3. **The state machine is the only way to change status.** Every transition goes through `transition()`, writes a status event with actor and note, is audited, and notifies the parties. Actors map to permissions: applicant = project owner; staff = `feasibility:manage`; expert = an active `ExpertAssignment` on that project.
4. **Questionnaires are data, not code.** The feasibility officer and the admin author templates and document lists per sector (sections, question types: text, long text, number with unit, single/multiple choice, date, table, file). A project pins the template version it started with, so later edits never change submitted answers. Answers are locked at submission and reopened only by `NEEDS_MORE_INFO`. A project may also carry project-specific questions, notes and required documents added by the applicant, the feasibility officer or the admin; each item records who added it.
5. **Access is per project.** Owner, staff with `feasibility:manage` and assigned experts only. Documents are private files of purpose `FEASIBILITY_DOCUMENT`, served by signed, expiring URLs; investors and the public never see them (OQ-05, OQ-07). Negative authorization tests cover every endpoint.
6. **The financial model lives in its own module (`financial-model`)**, linked to the project, edited by assigned experts, calculated by `@roshd/financial-engine`. The applicant may be invited to enter data in it (owner decision per project), but approval belongs to the expert.
7. **Commercial steps stay manual until decided.** Staff enter the cost estimate as numbers they decide (OQ-01). Contracting is "upload signed contract + staff confirms" (OQ-02). No online payment for studies until OQ-02 and OQ-09 are answered; the `orders` module can gain a `FEASIBILITY_STUDY` item kind later.
8. **Deliverables are immutable versions with two-step approval.** A deliverable follows the UNIDO chapter structure (configurable in the report template) and combines expert-written sections (Markdown), selected questionnaire answers and an **approved** financial-model run whose COMFAR schedules and, when done, economic analysis form the financial and economic chapters. It is approved first by the feasibility officer and then by an admin, in that order only; a rejection at either step returns it to drafting as a new version. Both approvals (name, time) appear on the report and in the audit log, and the applicant receives it only after the admin's approval. Rendering produces a Persian RTL PDF with self-hosted fonts on the server; each version is stored as a private file with its own hash. Delivered versions are never edited; a correction is a new version.
9. **Migration path.** Staff can convert an existing `FEASIBILITY` service request into a project (copying contact data and attachments), so Phase 1 requests are not lost.

## As built in ST-35.01

- **Tables.** `feasibility_projects` (code `FP-…`, applicant, title, sector, location, summary, status, optional `financialModelId` and `sourceRequestId`, both unique), `feasibility_status_events` (from, to, capacity of the actor, user, note) and `expert_assignments` (project, expert, who assigned and who ended it). The other entities of §1 arrive with their stories.
- **Permissions.** `feasibility:manage` (staff: every project, staff transitions, experts), `feasibility:work` (may be assigned as an expert; held by the `expert` role), `feasibility:approve-report` and `feasibility:final-approve`. The feasibility officer holds the first and the third; only admins and super admins hold the last.
- **One capacity on one's own project.** On a project they own, a user is the applicant and nothing else: staff rights do not count there and the applicant cannot be assigned as its expert. Elsewhere a user who is both staff and an assigned expert acts in the first capacity the rules accept, and that capacity is stored with the event.
- **Transitions.** `POST /api/v1/feasibility-projects/:id/transitions` is the only writer of `status`. The update is conditional on the status that was read and is written in one transaction with its event, so two concurrent requests cannot both apply a step. A step that does not exist is a 409, a step of another party a 403, and a project the caller has no relation to a 404. Status events cannot be updated in the database (trigger); they go only with their project. The note of a transition is addressed to the other parties and all of them read it; the applicant sees the capacity of who acted, staff and experts also see the name.
- **Preconditions of later steps.** The state machine decides who may take a step. What a step needs beyond that (a cost estimate before `COST_ESTIMATED`, a signed contract before `IN_PROGRESS`, the two approvals before `DELIVERED`) is checked in the same `transition()` by the stories that introduce those records; no web page offers these steps before then.
- **Experts.** Several experts can work on a project, all with the same rights; an assignment is active until it is ended and is kept as history afterwards. A lead-expert role is not modelled until a rule needs it. Access needs both the active assignment and the `feasibility:work` permission, and so does being told about a new status. Nobody assigns themselves; a user who is both staff and expert of a project (assigned by a colleague) can take the steps of both, which OQ-43 leaves to the owner. An assignment can be ended on an archived project too, to take access back.
- **Financial model.** The link is the foreign key on the project (`RESTRICT`): a model that belongs to a project cannot be deleted. Creating the model for a project and opening it to the assigned experts is ST-35.10; until then a model is still reached through its own owner and assignee (ADR-0009 §6).
- **Notifications.** A new status is announced to the applicant (in-app and e-mail), to the assigned experts, and to the staff when the applicant or an expert acted; never to the person who acted. A draft is announced to nobody. Announcements are best effort: a failure is logged and does not fail the transition.

## As built in ST-35.02

- **The applicant's draft.** While a project is a draft, and again when the staff ask for more information, its applicant changes the title, sector, location and description (`PATCH /api/v1/feasibility-projects/:id`); from the review on they stand as submitted. Staff read them and do not change them. A project is submitted only with a sector and a description, so that the reviewers know what it is about; the questionnaire adds its own conditions in its stories. The applicant deletes a draft they started themselves (`DELETE`); a project that was submitted once is kept with its history.
- **Conversion of a Phase 1 request (§9).** `POST /api/v1/feasibility-projects/from-request` needs `feasibility:manage` and `requests:read-all`. It makes a draft owned by the requester: the title is given by the staff member (a request has none), sector and location come from the request, its message becomes the description, and the first status event is recorded in the capacity of staff with the tracking code. The applicant is told and completes and submits the project like any other draft; they cannot delete it. A request becomes a project once (unique `sourceRequestId`). A request sent without an account has no applicant and is not converted (OQ-44); nobody converts their own request. The feasibility officer alone lacks `requests:read-all` (OQ-45).
- **Attachments move.** The files of the request are attached to the project in the same transaction (`FilesService.moveAttachments`), so they are listed on the project and no longer on the request. A file attached to a project is read by its owner and by holders of `feasibility:manage` (and `files:read-all`); assigned experts get the documents of a project through the project in ST-35.06, which also gives these files their own purpose; until then the project lists its files only for the applicant and the staff. The conversion leaves the status of the request as it is. These defaults are put to the owner in OQ-46.
- **Pages.** The applicant has «پروژه‌های امکان‌سنجی» in the dashboard (`/dashboard/feasibility`: list, new draft, project page with the timeline, change, submit, delete). Only the submission is offered there; the other steps of the applicant arrive with the records they need. Staff and experts have a read-only list and project page (`/dashboard/manage/feasibility`), which the notifications link to; the review steps and the assignment of experts are added to it by ST-35.07 and ST-35.10. The page of a feasibility request offers the conversion to holders of `feasibility:manage` and shows the project once it exists.

## As built in ST-35.03

- **Tables.** `questionnaire_templates` (title, sector or none for the general questionnaire, `isDemo`, archive date), `questionnaire_template_versions` (number, `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED`, the definition as JSON, who published it and when), `feasibility_projects.templateVersionId` (`RESTRICT`), `questionnaire_answers` (one row per project and question key) and `project_questionnaire_items` (a question, a document or a note of one project, with its origin).
- **A definition is data.** `questionnaireDefinitionSchema` of `@roshd/validation` describes sections, questions of eight types (text, long text, number with one unit or a choice of units, single and multiple choice, date, table with typed columns, file) and the documents to hand in. The same package checks an answer against its question (`validateAnswer`, `validateAnswers`), so the API and the form of the web app apply one rule. Numbers are decimal strings and are compared exactly.
- **Versions.** A template has at most one draft, which is edited freely (`PUT /api/v1/questionnaire-templates/:id/draft`) and published as the next version (`POST …/publish`); a questionnaire without a question is not published. A published version never changes and is never deleted; triggers in the database refuse both, also past the API. A correction is the next version: the first save after a publication starts it, and it can be dropped again while a published version exists. An archived template is not given to new projects and is not edited until it is taken out of the archive. All template routes need `feasibility:manage`.
- **Pinning.** The applicant starts the questionnaire of a project (`POST /api/v1/feasibility-projects/:id/questionnaire/start`), which pins the project to the latest published version of the questionnaire of its sector, or of the general one when the sector has none; when several active templates fit, the one published last (OQ-47). A version published later, a change of the sector and the archive of the template do not reach a project that has started.
- **Answers.** Only the applicant answers (`PUT …/questionnaire/answers`), a few questions at a time and with gaps; an empty value takes an answer back, and one wrong answer refuses the whole request. Whoever sees the project (applicant, staff, assigned expert) reads the questionnaire with its answers. Answers are saved only while the project is a draft or more information is asked for (409 otherwise). The submission of a project checks under the same row lock that every required question is answered and every answer still satisfies its question (400 with one detail per question, path `answers.<key>`).
- **A project without a questionnaire is still submitted.** Starting the questionnaire is not yet a condition of the submission, because the applicant has no page for it before ST-35.05; whether it becomes one is OQ-47.
- **Items of one project (OQ-36).** `POST …/questionnaire/items` adds a question, a document or a note to one project, and `DELETE …/items/:itemId` takes it out again with its answer. The applicant adds and removes while they may answer; staff (`feasibility:manage`, never on a project of their own) until the intake review is over (`DRAFT` to `NEEDS_MORE_INFO`), and the applicant is told. Each side removes only what it added, and the origin (`applicant` or `staff`) stays with the item. The API gives the key (`item_…`), a prefix no template may use, so the keys of a template and of a project never meet. A question of a project passes the same consistency checks as one of a template (no range nothing fits into, no repeated options or columns), so that it can always be answered. Each side adds at most 50 items to a project, and neither can use up the share of the other. What is removed stays readable in the audit log, with a note that it had an answer but without the answer itself. A required question the staff add during the review is asked for when the project is submitted again, not before the next step of the staff (OQ-47). An assigned expert reads the items and adds none.
- **Files.** An answer to a file question and a document of the list are only described here: the answer holds file ids whose shape is checked. Uploading, ownership and access to these files arrive with ST-35.06.
- **Content from the bank's guide.** The owner decided on 2026-10-03 that the questionnaires may follow the structure and the wording of the bank's guide for feasibility reports (kept in the git-ignored `references/`), but never carry the bank's name, logo or form code. The sample template of ST-35.04 and every later template follow this.

## As built in ST-35.04

- **Pages.** Holders of `feasibility:manage` have «قالب‌های پرسشنامه» in the dashboard (`/dashboard/manage/questionnaires`): the list of templates (active or archived) with the state of their versions, a form for a new template (title and sector, or general), and the page of one template with its facts (title, sector, archive), the editor of its content, a preview and the list of its versions.
- **The editor works on the draft.** It starts from the draft, or from the published version when there is none, and changes nothing on the server until «ذخیره پیش‌نویس»; the first save after a publication starts the next version. «انتشار نسخه» saves what is unsaved, asks for confirmation and publishes; «کنار گذاشتن پیش‌نویس» goes back to the published version. Closing or reloading the page with unsaved changes asks first; a link inside the dashboard does not yet. An archived template is shown and cannot be edited.
- **Keyboard.** Sections, questions, table columns and documents are added, moved up and down and removed with ordinary buttons whose names say what they act on («بالا بردن سؤال ۲ از بخش ۱»). The focus stays with the part that moves, goes to its neighbour (or to the add button) when it is removed, and a button that cannot act (the first part cannot go up, a table keeps its last column) is marked `aria-disabled` and stays focusable; every move, addition and removal is announced in a live region. There is no drag and drop.
- **Keys are not typed.** The editor gives every section, question, option, column and document its key when it is added (`s1`, `q1`, `o1`, `c1`, `d1`) and never changes it afterwards, so relabelling or moving a part does not make it another one; a new key is one past the highest in use. Options and units are written one per line and applied as they are typed; one unit is a fixed unit and several are a choice, and an option keeps its value when lines above it are added or removed. Switching a question or a column to another type and back gives it back what it had (columns, options, units), as long as the page is not reloaded or saved in between; between the two kinds of choice the options simply go along. While a save or a publication is on its way the editor does not take input, so that the answer of the server cannot overwrite it.
- **Errors.** The definition is checked in the browser with the schema of the API before it is sent, and what only the API finds is shown the same way: the message with the place in the words of the editor («بخش «…»، سؤال ۲»).
- **Preview.** `QuestionnairePreview` draws the questionnaire as the applicant will read it (sections, questions with their type, units, options and table columns, and the documents) without working fields. The form the applicant fills in is ST-35.05.
- **Sample template.** The demo seed (`pnpm --filter @roshd/api db:seed:demo`, never in production) publishes one general questionnaire marked `isDemo`, which the pages show with «نمونه نمایشی». Its sections follow the usual structure of a feasibility report (record of the applicant, shareholders, management, permits, product and project) and carry no name, logo or form code of a bank. The seed adds it only while there is no sample template. The real templates per sector are written by the feasibility officer and the admin (OQ-36).
- **Not in the editor yet.** The limits of a question beyond its unit and range (length of a text, number of choices, rows or files, range of a date, range of a table column) are kept when a definition that has them is edited, and are neither shown nor editable; a later story adds them.

## As built in ST-35.05

- **Page.** The applicant fills in the questionnaire of a project at `/dashboard/feasibility/[id]/questionnaire`; the project page shows a card with the progress and the way there, and when a submission is refused because of the questionnaire it points there too. Before the questionnaire is started the page offers «شروع پرسشنامه» (when a published version exists for the project) and says that the project keeps the version it starts with.
- **Steps.** One step per section of the pinned version, and a last step «موارد اختصاصی و مدارک» for what belongs to this project only: the questions, documents and notes added by the applicant or by staff (each marked with its origin) and the list of documents of the template. The form opens at the first step that still needs something, the steps are reached by their buttons in any order, and a new step is read from its title on. A bar and a line of text say how many questions are answered and how many required ones are open, by what is saved.
- **Saving.** An answer is queued when it changes and sent a moment later or when its field is left (`PUT …/questionnaire/answers` with only the answers that changed). It leaves the queue only when the server has it: an answer typed while a request was on its way, or lost by the network or the server, is sent again (a few times by itself, then with «تلاش دوباره برای ذخیره»), and when the API refuses a request for some answers the others of that request are sent again. What is still queued when the page is left, by a link inside the dashboard or by closing it, goes out with a last request (one that outlives the page when the page is closed, as long as it is small enough for that); closing the page with unsent answers also asks first. If the project was submitted meanwhile the page turns read-only. Every changed answer is checked in the browser with `validateAnswer` of `@roshd/validation`, the rule of the API: what does not fit its question stays in the form with its Persian message next to the field and is not sent, the line under the form says how many answers need correcting or completing, and what only the API refuses is shown at the same place.
- **Completeness.** Gaps are fine while filling in. «بررسی کامل بودن پرسشنامه» applies the check of the submission to what is saved (required questions, required cells and the least number of rows of a table), marks every open place with its message and opens the first step that has one; the link of a refused submission on the project page opens the questionnaire with this check already done.
- **Controls.** Text, long text, number (with its fixed unit beside it or a list of units to choose from), single and multiple choice, date (the Jalali date input of `@roshd/ui`) and table. A table answer is a list of rows, each a small form with a labelled field per column, so it reads the same on a phone and needs no sideways scrolling; rows are added and removed with buttons. A question of type file says how many files it has and that they are handed in in the last step, with the documents of the project (ST-35.06).
- **Own items.** In the last step the applicant adds a note, a question (text or number) or a document to their own project and removes what they added; an item of the staff is answered and cannot be removed by the applicant.
- **Locked.** When the project is submitted the same page shows the answers without letting them be changed, and says that they open again if more information is asked for.
- **Not yet.** Staff and experts read the answers through the API; their page for it comes with the intake review (ST-35.07). Whether a project must have a started and complete questionnaire to be submitted is still OQ-47.

## As built in ST-35.06

- **What can be handed in.** The documents of a project are what its questionnaire asks for: the documents of the pinned template, the documents the project has of its own (added by the applicant or by staff, ST-35.03), and the file questions of both. There is no free upload beside them; an applicant who wants to hand in something else adds a document of their own first. `GET /api/v1/feasibility-projects/:id/documents` lists these places with their files.
- **Table and files.** `project_documents` holds one row per file (project, kind `DOCUMENT` or `ANSWER`, the key of the document or file question, version, file). The bytes are a `files` row with the new purpose `FEASIBILITY_DOCUMENT`, private, attached to the project (`entityType` `feasibility_project`) from the moment they are stored, so they are never a loose upload and do not count against the upload quota; instead a document takes at most 20 versions, a file question as many files as its `maxFiles`, a project 300 files in all, and all documents of one applicant over all their projects at most 1 GB (413 beyond that), so that one account cannot fill the disk. Type and size are checked as for every upload (allowlist with content sniffing, 8 MB).
- **Versions, never overwriting.** Handing in a document again adds its next version; the earlier versions stay and are listed, newest first. (The number of a version that was taken back before it was ever submitted is given again; the audit log tells such files apart by their id.) A file question takes several files, and its answer in the questionnaire is the list of their ids, written only by the uploads: `PUT …/questionnaire/answers` refuses a file question, so an id that was not checked can never become an answer.
- **Who does what.** Only the applicant hands in (`POST …/documents`, multipart with `kind` and `key`) and only while the project is a draft or more information is asked for (409 otherwise). The applicant may take back a file handed in by mistake (`DELETE …/documents/:documentId`), but only one added since the last submission: what the reviewers were sent stays, and a correction is a new version. For the same reason an item of a project (a document or a file question the applicant or staff added) cannot be removed any more once a file of it was submitted (409). Staff and experts hand in nothing here.
- **Reading.** Whoever sees the project — its applicant, staff with `feasibility:manage`, an expert while assigned — reads the list and gets a signed, expiring URL per file (`POST …/documents/:documentId/download-url`); for everyone else the project does not exist (404). The file routes of the `files` module give an assigned expert nothing: the access of an expert exists only through the project and ends with the assignment (a signed URL that was already issued works until it expires, at most an hour by configuration). Staff with `feasibility:manage` can also open a document through the file routes, and holders of `files:read-all` (admins) see and can delete it in the staff file browser like every other file. A document deleted there is no longer a version of the project: it is not listed, cannot be downloaded and does not count as handed in. All documents are private (the temporary answer to OQ-07).
- **Submission.** A required document of the list without a file refuses the submission like an open required question (400 with `documents.<key>` and a message that names the document); the questionnaire of a project also says which required documents are missing (`missingDocuments`), so that the form can show it before the applicant submits.
- **Going with what it belongs to.** Removing an item of a project removes the files handed in for it; deleting a draft removes all its files. The rows go in the same transaction, the bytes right after, and every file removed is audited. A file is stored before its row is written; if the row never comes to be, or bytes cannot be removed at once, the scheduled sweep of the files module removes documents that have no row.
- **Pages.** The last step of the applicant's questionnaire page lists the documents and the files of file questions with their versions, a button to hand in (a new version), download and — where allowed — «برداشتن»; a file question in the form says how many files it has and where they are handed in; the check for completeness counts missing documents. Staff and assigned experts read and download the same list on the project page of the dashboard.
- **Defaults put to the owner (OQ-48).** That only the applicant hands in documents, that a submitted file cannot be taken back, and the limits above.

## As built in ST-35.07

- **Review queue.** `GET /api/v1/feasibility-projects?scope=all&queue=review` (staff with `feasibility:manage`) lists what waits for the intake — the projects that are `SUBMITTED` or in `INITIAL_REVIEW` — with the longest waiting first; a status narrows it further. The staff list of the dashboard opens on this queue («صف بررسی») and keeps the whole list and the expert's own list as its other views.
- **Steps of the review.** The state machine and `POST …/transitions` are those of ST-35.01; the project page of the staff now offers the steps of the intake that the API gives the caller: start the review (`INITIAL_REVIEW`), ask for more information (`NEEDS_MORE_INFO`) and archive (`ARCHIVED`). The step to the cost estimate is not offered before its own story.
- **Explained steps.** When staff ask for more information or archive a project, the note is required (400 with `note`); only filing a delivered study away needs none. The applicant reads the note on the timeline of the project, and the notification of the new status carries it (its first 300 characters) instead of the title of the project. The steps of the applicant and of experts stay as they were.
- **Reopening.** `NEEDS_MORE_INFO` opens the whole project for its applicant again: the details, every answer of the questionnaire, the project's own items and the documents (new versions and new files; what was submitted stays). The note says which of them the reviewers mean; answers are not opened one by one. The resubmission checks the questionnaire and the required documents again.
- **Reading the answers.** Staff and assigned experts read the questionnaire of a project with the applicant's answers at `/dashboard/manage/feasibility/[id]/questionnaire` — the form of the applicant, locked, in the words of a reader — together with the documents. While the intake lasts, staff add their own question, document or note there (ST-35.03), and the applicant is told.
- **Not here.** Automatic archiving of a project that stays in `NEEDS_MORE_INFO` (the `system` actor of the state machine) is not scheduled yet; how long to wait is the owner's to say (OQ-49).

## Consequences

- The applicant and the company work in one place with a full, audited history.
- More tables and a PDF renderer in the API image (fonts and a headless renderer or a PDF library; chosen in the deliverable story by measured image size and Persian shaping quality).
- Business decisions slot in without schema rewrites: pricing and contracts are already explicit steps.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep extending `ServiceRequest`.** Its status model and fields cannot hold questionnaires, experts and deliverables. Rejected.
- **Hard-coded questionnaire per sector.** Every content change would need a deploy. Rejected.
- **Generating the report as an editable Word file only.** Not tamper-evident and hard to version. Word export may be added later; PDF is the delivered artefact.
